# Academic evidence behind most of the Innate design

Most technical choices in the Innate plan have a peer-reviewed paper behind them. PIN typing rhythm plus touch offset tells one person from another, with **EER 3.65–7%** on a soft PIN keypad ([Zheng 2014](https://www.eecis.udel.edu/~hnw/paper/tapping.pdf)). A template score of the "distance divided by variability" kind is the best detector of 14 on a standard keystroke benchmark ([Killourhy & Maxion 2009](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~keystroke/KillourhyMaxion09.pdf)). Bots that draw hand-crafted interactions are caught almost perfectly, while GAN-generated bots are much harder ([Acien 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13655)). Splitting by time, using one phone, and keeping impostor data out of the training set are exactly the three "traps" that FETA measured ([Georgiev 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10606)). Risk-based step-up authentication is a pattern already validated at the scale of millions of users ([Wiefling 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15139)). There are three places where the evidence is weak, and they touch the demo story directly. First, 3–5 enrollment sessions in a single sitting is far fewer than in any of the source papers. Second, the "being coached" signal has only security-vendor marketing material behind it and no academic study, so it cannot serve as the "more stable" fallback at the 28/11 milestone. Third, the signature of real Accessibility malware ("taps exactly at the center, rhythm too regular") has never been measured. The plan should be corrected on about ten specific points (see the last section), mainly the enrollment protocol, how the template is updated, how numbers are reported, and how limitations are described.

Every PDF path in this report is relative to the repo root and was checked against the real file list in `papers/` (on 23/11/2026).

## PIN plus touch offset is strong enough, but 3–5 enrollment sessions is not

The core claim of Model 2 stands on solid ground. Zheng et al. measured more than 80 people and 11,062 PIN entries and recorded an average EER **as low as 3.65%**. Combining all four feature groups (timing, pressure, touch size, acceleration) always beats each group alone. PINs with repeated digits such as 1-1-1-1 are less accurate ([Zheng 2014](https://www.eecis.udel.edu/~hnw/paper/tapping.pdf)). Buschek adds the piece the plan relies on: adding the offset from the key center to the hold/release times **lowers EER by 26.4–36.8%** versus timing alone. The offset is the most important addition ([Buschek 2015](http://www.florian-alt.org/unibw/wp-content/publications/buschek2015chi.pdf)). So the phrase "PIN rhythm is strongest" in §5.0 should become "rhythm combined with touch position is strongest". Version 1 is also directly supported: scaled Manhattan (the template mean, each dimension divided by its variability) ranks first of 14 detectors with **EER 9.6%** ([Killourhy & Maxion 2009](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~keystroke/KillourhyMaxion09.pdf)). Zheng uses exactly the L1-divided-by-σ formula ([Zheng 2014](https://www.eecis.udel.edu/~hnw/paper/tapping.pdf)). Each term in the sum is one feature's contribution, so an explanation such as "the PIN was 40% slower" is available without adding a model. One caveat from the same results table: at the operating point that lets no impostor through, scaled Manhattan drops to 5th place. Detector ranking depends on the operating point.

The gap is in the number of enrollment sessions. Zheng's 3.65% uses **85 training sessions per person**, and accuracy only plateaus around 20 sessions ([Zheng 2014](https://www.eecis.udel.edu/~hnw/paper/tapping.pdf)). Buschek shows that training and testing in the same session gives an EER less than half that of across sessions, and so recommends collecting data over at least two sessions ([Buschek 2015](http://www.florian-alt.org/unibw/wp-content/publications/buschek2015chi.pdf)). Pretrained shared models tolerate fewer sessions: TypeNet on mobile goes from **10.7% (G=1) down to 7.3% (G=5)** ([Acien 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05570)), the Transformer from 6.99% to 3.84% ([Stragapede 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07596)). But those models learn from more than 100k people, while Innate's Version 2 has only about 25. The demo script for judge A enrolls 3–5 sessions in a row and then tests immediately. That is the "within one session" condition, the optimistic scenario Buschek warns about. The demo may therefore run well on stage while the across-session numbers on the slides are much worse. The team should state this difference plainly instead of letting the judges discover it. The plan also has no evidence yet for swipe/scroll features in a short wallet session. Touchalytics needs about **11–12 swipe strokes** to reach 2–3% EER, and a single stroke gives about 13% ([Frank 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.6231)). HMOG measures plain tap/key hold/swipe at **25.7–34.2% EER** ([Sitová 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01199)). Swiping should be a secondary signal for Model 2, as the plan states, and mainly serves Model 1.

The rule "update the template only after a high-matching session" is the standard self-update technique, but it is not safe enough. Biggio shows that a crafted sequence of samples, each of which passes the update threshold, can **gradually poison** the template until the impostor is accepted ([Biggio 2012](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-642-34166-3_46.pdf)). Lovisotto measures a white-box attack that **succeeds more than 70% with fewer than 10 injections**. The countermeasure is to detect a consistent drift direction across updates, which catches more than 99% after two injections. That paper also names "supervised update" (requiring re-authentication before an update) as the safer option ([Lovisotto 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09162)). Both studies were on faces, not PIN rhythm. No poisoning study on mobile touch templates exists, so this is an inference by transfer.

## Catching simple bots is easy; bots that learn from real people are not

The premise of scenario A has a firm academic basis. Cloak and Dagger shows that the Accessibility permission combined with the draw-over permission gives malware **full control of the UI feedback loop**, and Google classified these flaws as won't-fix ([Fratantonio 2017](https://cloak-and-dagger.org/)). Merely enabling one Accessibility service exposes login credentials of **72% of top financial apps** to eavesdropping ([Naseri 2019](https://petsymposium.org/2019/files/papers/issue2/popets-2019-0031.pdf)). Abuse of the Accessibility API is widespread, and Accessibility events carry no integrity guarantee ([Diao 2019](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/raid2019-diao.pdf)). Security vendor reports confirm RedHook targeting Vietnam through a fake State Bank page ([Cyble](https://cyble.com/blog/redhook-new-android-banking-targeting-in-vietnam/)), and Gigabud using Accessibility to tap on its own ([Group-IB](https://www.group-ib.com/blog/gigabud-banking-malware/)). Even so, **no paper measures the rhythm or geometry of touches injected in the wild by Accessibility malware**. "Rhythm too regular, taps exactly at the button center" remains an inference, not a measurement.

The 4-tactic ladder of §5.1 matches the difficulty curve in the literature almost exactly. Web bots moving the mouse in fixed 8 px steps are caught **100% from the first requests** ([Iliou 2021a](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/36055/1/3447815.pdf)). On phones, using touch alone BeCAPTCHA reaches about **60%** against GAN bots, and GAN trajectories are "nearly impossible" to separate by touch. Adding the accelerometer raises this to **80–90%**, and training on both bot generators gives above 90% ([Acien 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13655)). For the mouse, neuromotor features reach 93% from one trajectory ([Acien 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00890)). For typing rhythm, synthetic keystrokes are caught well when labeled data is plentiful, but few-shot is a "major challenge" ([DeAlcala 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13394)). Tactic 4 has a clear precedent: a Lego robot driven by the population's swipe statistics reached an "alarmingly high" penetration rate ([Serwadda & Phoha 2013](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/When-kids'-toys-breach-mobile-phone-security-Serwadda-Phoha/5c3556d4bf94ba51cea58c5d624aed19e6223e59)). GAN bots evade detection even when the server knows the attack method ([Iliou 2021b](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/36301/1/Web_bot_detection_evasion_using_Generative_Adversarial_Networks_R1_v1.pdf)). The ActReal preprint shows system-level agents faking even the IMU and still **succeeding 71.1%** against a detector combining touch with IMU ([Wang 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30038)).

Two consequences follow. First, the IMU is the most-cited way to raise accuracy. Ordinary Accessibility malware taps on a phone lying still, with no hand tremor, so the IMU is worth collecting (through DeviceMotion on the web). This is an inference: ActReal only proves that system-level agents can fake the IMU, while ordinary malware does not. Second, the "hacker console" in the demo taps from another machine through the team's own emulator. A model trained on bots the team generated itself risks learning the emulator's traces instead of real malware's traces. There should be at least one more realistic attack source, for example an Android app using `dispatchGesture` or the ADB command `input tap`. This recommendation is an inference from the evidence gap described above.

## FETA's three traps were avoided, but the way numbers are reported is missing

§5.5 is the best-supported part of the plan. FETA measured 515 people over 31 days. They found that all 30 reviewed touch papers committed at least one trap. Putting impostor data into training improves EER by **2.55 percentage points**, non-contiguous splits by **3.8**, mixing phone models by **3.2–5.8**. Combined, a realistic EER of **13.8%** becomes an "unrealistic" **4.9%** ([Georgiev 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10606)). Innate's three choices (split by time, one phone, no random split) avoid exactly those three traps, and the slides should use the 13.8% versus 4.9% figures to explain why Innate's numbers are "worse" than the literature but honest. Touchalytics once suspected that a too-good mouse result was because each person used one machine ([Frank 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.6231)). BehavePassDB also criticizes the one-device-per-person protocol ([Stragapede 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02502)). In exchange, using one phone means the judges' question "what if I change phones?" must be answered with "enroll again". BB-MAS shows that key hold times and inter-key latencies change by device ([Belman 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02736)).

The reporting of numbers is still missing. Eberz points out that mean EER hides systematic errors: some impostors "always get through". Blindly optimizing mean EER can reduce security. They propose reporting the error distribution per person, using the Gini coefficient ([Eberz 2017](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/14899/asiaccs17.pdf)). Sugrim advises reporting full ROC curves with score distributions ([Sugrim 2019](http://jannelindqvist.com/publications/NDSS19-robustmetrics.pdf)). According to Ahmed & Imtiaz, ISO/IEC 19795-1 requires error at the operating point, DET curves, and **uncertainty intervals for every value** ([Ahmed 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20680), preprint). So the plan's "FAR at FRR = 5%" is the right direction and only lacks a DET curve and bootstrap confidence intervals. With about 25 people there are few impostor comparisons, so the confidence intervals will be wide and must be shown. Also, sessions of "knows the PIN, impersonates the owner" must sit only in the test set. Version 2 must be validated leave-users-out, like KVC's open-set protocol ([Stragapede 2023 KVC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06000)). Finally, the impostor in the demo is an unpracticed person (zero-effort). Khan shows that after only about 4 minutes of practice with an AR application, an impostor gets through **87%** of attempts ([Khan 2018](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~uhengart/publications/mobisys18.pdf)). Collecting data over about 3 days also only measures short-term drift, not aging over weeks ([Teh 2016](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/files/33082822/COSE_Preprint_escholar.pdf)).

For external datasets, HuMIdb (600 people, 14 sensors, with a bot benchmark) is closest to Model 1 ([Acien 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13655)). Aalto/KVC is the choice for cross-checking mobile typing rhythm ([Stragapede 2023 KVC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06000)). None uses a custom-drawn PIN keypad through Pointer Events, so external numbers are for reference only. The license of each dataset has not been checked; it must be read before use and recorded in the README.

## Step-up has large-scale evidence; "hold" and "being coached" do not

The three-zone design has its closest precedent in Freeman. That paper scores the trustworthiness of a login per person, combines signals with learned weights, and sets thresholds to decide step-up. The weights matter: AUC rises from **0.913 to 0.955**. The system blocks 95% of botnets at a 10% false-alarm rate ([Freeman 2016](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/who-are-you-statistical-approach-measuring-user-authenticity.pdf)). Users rated RBA easier to use than always-on 2FA while feeling equally secure. That is evidence for the "Human Centered" angle ([Wiefling 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00339)). Small configuration changes can change security and usability a lot, so thresholds A/B must be adjustable and not fixed ([Wiefling 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10681)). At the scale of 3.3 million users, users benefit from RBA "after just a few logins", and parameters can be optimized by ML. That is exactly the "replace with a supervised model" path ([Wiefling 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15139)). On score normalization, the classic approach is to normalize (min-max, z-score or tanh) and then add. tanh is robust to outliers, which suits enrollment statistics with only 3–5 noisy samples ([Jain 2005](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Score-normalization-in-multimodal-biometric-systems-Jain-Nandakumar/0a00f3ca6efe78953b40c68ad13fbd1b01a1d4e6), paywalled, abstract only).

Evidence is missing in three places. First, RBA studies use login context (IP, browser), not transaction context. **No paper fuses touch biometrics with transaction features** such as a new recipient or an unusual amount. Real mobile wallet fraud data is also scarce even in academia, which is why PaySim is needed ([Lopez-Rojas 2016](https://www.msc-les.org/proceedings/emss/2016/EMSS2016_249.pdf)). Second, the `hold` zone is a design extension: RBA studies usually have only two zones. Third, the "being coached" signal rests only on BioCatch and ThreatFabric material ([BioCatch](https://www.biocatch.com/blog/authorized-push-payment-fraud-how-digital-behavior-can-uncover-social-engineering-scams), [ThreatFabric](https://www.threatfabric.com/blogs/a-multi-layered-approach-to-detecting-impersonation-scams)), with no academic study or public figure. This directly contradicts the 28/11 milestone option, which treats "being coached" as a "more stable" signal.

The explanation layer holds up. SHAP is the only additive attribution that satisfies the three axioms ([Lundberg 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07874)). TreeSHAP computes exactly in polynomial time for LightGBM ([Lundberg 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04610)), so per-request explanation stays within the 200 ms budget. With a linear score function, each term w·x is already an exact contribution, with no need for SHAP. SHAP results depend on the background set, so it must be fixed and documented ([Psychoula 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06314)). The plan's constraint on `pressure` matches the W3C spec. A device that does not support pressure must return `pressure` = 0.5, and when contact area cannot be measured `width`/`height` default to 1 ([W3C Pointer Events 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/pointerevents3/)). That means the "touch area" feature in §5.0 may also be just a placeholder value. The spec also lets scripts create pointer events themselves, so ignoring `isTrusted` is correct. The principle "the LLM is not on the decision path" has no paper of its own to verify it. It is a reasonable engineering argument and needs no citation.

## Conclusion

The academic evidence for Innate is strongest on **how to measure**, not on **the expected numbers**. The team's evaluation protocol is already better than most papers in the field. So the real presentation advantage is saying up front that the numbers will be worse than the literature and explaining why. That framing is much harder for a technical judge to pick apart than a pretty EER.

The biggest risk is not in the model. It is that the demo measures one thing (enrolling and testing in the same session, an unpracticed impostor, bots the team generated itself) while the slides claim another. In addition, the fallback at the 28/11 milestone relies on the one signal with no academic evidence at all. Fixing items 7, 8 and 9 in the table above would close that gap at the lowest cost.
